# The Last Train

The station was nearly empty when Sarah arrived. Only a few late-night travelers sat scattered across the worn wooden benches, their faces illuminated by the harsh fluorescent lights overhead.

She checked her watch again. 11:47 PM. The last train to Portland would arrive in thirteen minutes. She had been waiting for this moment all day, rehearsing what she would say when she finally saw him again.

The cold November wind whipped through the open platform, and Sarah pulled her coat tighter around herself.

A voice crackled over the intercom: "The 12:00 AM train to Portland has been delayed by approximately fifteen minutes. We apologize for any inconvenience."

Sarah sighed and sat down on the nearest bench. The metal was freezing cold against her legs. She pulled out her phone and stared at the last message from David: "I'll be on the midnight train. We need to talk."

Those five words had been echoing in her mind for hours. "We need to talk." Nothing good ever followed that phrase. She knew what was comming. After three years together, she could read him like a book.

A young mother with a crying baby sat down on the bench across from her. The woman looked exhausted, dark circles under her eyes, trying desperately to soothe the infant.

Sarah watched them for a moment, then looked away. She used to imagine having a family with David. Now those dreams seemed foolish and distant.

The platform began to fill with more people as midnight approached. Business travelers in rumpled suits, college students with overstuffed backpacks, an elderly couple holding hands.

Finally, the distant sound of the train whistle cut through the night air. Sarah stood up, her heart pounding. This was it. In moments, she would see David's face and know the truth.

The train pulled into the station with a screech of brakes. Doors opened. Passengers began to disembark.

And there he was.
