# The Last Train

The station was nearly deserted when Sarah arrived, just a few late-night travelers scattered across the worn wooden benches, their weary faces illuminated by harsh fluorescent lights.

She checked her watch again: 11:47 PM. The last train to Portland would arrive in thirteen minutes. She'd been waiting for this moment all day, rehearsing what she would say when she finally saw him.

A voice crackled over the intercom: "The 12:00 AM train to Portland has been delayed by approximately fifteen minutes. We apologize for any inconvenience."

Sarah sighed heavily and sat down on the nearest bench, the cold metal biting through her jeans. She pulled out her phone and stared at David's last message: "I'll be on the midnight train. We need to talk."

Those five words had been echoing in her mind all evening. "We need to talk." Nothing good ever came after that phrase. After three years together, she could read him perfectly, and she knew what was coming.

The cold November wind whipped through the open platform. Sarah pulled her coat tighter, but it did nothing to warm the chill settling in her chest.

A young mother with a crying baby sat down across from her. The woman looked utterly exhausted, dark circles shadowing her eyes as she desperately tried to soothe the infant. Sarah watched them briefly, then looked away, remembering all those times she'd imagined having a family with David. Those dreams seemed foolish now, like they belonged to someone else entirely.

As midnight approached, the platform filled with more travelers: business people in rumpled suits clutching briefcases, college students with overstuffed backpacks and headphones, an elderly couple holding hands and whispering to each other.

The distant sound of the train whistle pierced the night. Sarah stood up quickly, her heart hammering against her ribs. This was it.

The train pulled into the station with a metallic screech. Doors hissed open. Passengers began streaming out onto the platform, a river of tired faces and rolling luggage.

And there he was, standing in the doorway of the third car, searching the crowd. When his eyes found hers, Sarah knew immediately that she'd been right about everything.
